# Open Question 5: T-Duality and the Total Group of Periods

> **Date Added:** January 12, 2026

### Context

T-Duality is a symmetry in String Theory that relates a theory on a circle of radius $R$ to one on radius $1/R$. It exchanges momentum modes (geometric data) with winding modes (topological data).
*   **Fact:** In standard geometric quantization, these two types of data are treated separately (curvature integrality vs. flat bundles).
*   **Fact:** The "Total Group of Periods" $\P_\omega$, introduced in the context of the Prequantum Groupoid [1], explicitly unifies spherical periods (momentum) and toric/surfacic periods (winding) into a single subgroup of $\mathbb{R}$.

### The Problem

T-Duality is usually formulated as a relationship between two distinct classical descriptions that yield the same quantum physics. We lack a geometric formulation of this equivalence at the prequantum level that treats geometry and topology on equal footing.

*   **Question:** Can T-Duality be formulated as a natural isomorphism between the **Prequantum Groupoids** $\TT_\omega(X)$ and $\TT_{\omega^\vee}(X^\vee)$? Is the "Total Torus of Periods" $\T_\omega = \mathbb{R}/\P_\omega$ the fundamental invariant under T-duality, suggesting that T-duality is a symmetry of the *Quantum System* (the groupoid) that is invisible in the classical Skeleton?
