# Does the motor cortex draw on a wire plane? A diffeological model of the Two-Thirds Power Law

> **Author:** Patrick Iglesias-Zemmour
> **Status:** Preprint (2025) — Revised version (2026), under review at *Biological Cybernetics*
> **Keywords:** Diffeology, Wire Diffeology, Two-Thirds Power Law, Equi-affine Geometry, Motor Control, Motor Primitives

## Abstract

The Two-Thirds Power Law of human motor control ($`v \propto \kappa^{-1/3}`$) is geometrically equivalent to constant equi-affine speed. In classical differential geometry, however, the equi-affine metric is not a tensor: it depends on acceleration, which does not transform covariantly under arbitrary coordinate changes. To recover tensorial behavior, one must either restrict the symmetry group to the affine group or introduce an affine connection — sacrificing full diffeomorphism covariance.

This paper proposes a different geometric setting. We equip the Euclidean plane with the *wire diffeology*, the smooth structure generated by all smooth curves. In this diffeological space, the equi-affine metric becomes a true covariant 3-tensor under the *full* diffeomorphism group — no restriction of symmetries, no additional structure required.

The construction is motivated by a simple fact: the motor cortex traces curves, not two-dimensional patches. Accordingly, curves are taken as primitive, echoing the motor control literature in which movements are built from a repertoire of elementary building blocks — motor primitives. The wire plane offers a geometric formalization of this idea in which the Two-Thirds Power Law emerges as a fully covariant invariant.

The paper is a mathematical model, not a neural hypothesis. It establishes that a fully covariant geometric formulation exists, and that this formulation aligns naturally with the motor primitives framework.

## Significance

The standard geometric interpretation of the Two-Thirds Power Law faces a mathematical obstruction. The equi-affine metric is not a tensor under arbitrary diffeomorphisms of the plane. The available fixes — restricting to the affine subgroup or introducing an affine connection — do not restore full diffeomorphism covariance. They manage the problem; they do not resolve it.

This paper shows that a different choice of geometry — the wire diffeology — restores full covariance. The equi-affine tensor becomes intrinsic to the smooth structure, with no restriction of symmetries and no additional structure. This is something the standard framework cannot achieve.

Moreover, because the wire diffeology is built from curves, it provides a natural geometric home for the motor primitives framework of Flash and colleagues. The Two-Thirds Power Law emerges not as an imposed constraint, but as an intrinsic property of a geometry generated by the movements themselves.
